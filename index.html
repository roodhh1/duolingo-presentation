The challenge — judgment under pressure

Executive pressure met a counter-intuitive signal

When the IRS opened in late January, TurboTax was struggling on paying customers company-wide — and the pressure to fix it was immediate.

  • What I found: users steering toward higher tiers were converting worst in that early window.
  • The honest read: some of that is self-selection — so I didn't just correlate, I had us test routing against the control to confirm it moved conversion.
◆ My contribution #2 — the mid-season call
⚡ Extrinsic pressure

"Fix paying-customer volume — now." A whole-company problem landing on our channel.

My decision

During "first peak" — the high-volume, refund-driven, price-sensitive week after IRS open — route toward lower tiers to win the paying customer. A reversible experiment, decided on evidence, not on panic.

Strategy across the season

One policy, inverted across the season

Different customers arrive at different times. So the routing policy flipped — deliberately — to balance volume early and value late.

  • Early (first peak): refund-driven, price-sensitive → route lower → win paying customers.
  • Late season: complex, balance-due, less price-sensitive → route higher → capture value.

Managing the funnel as a portfolio over time is what let us hit the growth target and the revenue guardrail.

Route lower early (win units) → route higher late (capture revenue).
How we knew it was real

A control ran all season — so every gain was measured, not assumed

  • Season-long control: the prior, longer flow — the standard screens a cold TurboTax user sees. Every recipe measured against it.
  • Bayesian reads, pre-set risk threshold — fast decisions without peeking into false wins.
  • Guardrails watched: revenue per customer, abandonment, downstream completion.
The result that matters most

Winning recipes held S2C above index 102 vs control all season — we tripled starts and improved conversion. The quality trap never sprang.
